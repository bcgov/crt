# Project Overview

## What is Capital Rehabilitation Projects Tracking Database?

Various parties of the rehabilitation projects team use the current application - SharePoint (OCIO hosted) front end and Access database - for data entry. These parties are usually ministry project managers from various districts of the province, though other staff members may also perform this action. This tool is then used for reporting purposes. This SharePoint is due to be updated and moved to SQL server 2016, this will deprecate the application. This application is important to the business area, as it is a significant source of reporting for rehabilitation projects. This data is used by HQ as well the districts for various purposes, such as planning and monitoring expenses, tracking accomplishments, creating dashboards etc.

The capability of this application is intended to be included in the Integrated Projects System (IPS) once it is live. Since IPS is not under development yet (November 2020), an alternate solution needs to be made available for the business area to ensure business continuity after the SharePoint is upgraded. This project (Capital Rehabilitation Projects Tracking Database, CRT) will deliver the new application on a technology stack that adheres to MoTI standards and can be supported by MoTI IMB staff.

## Who uses the Capital Rehabilitation Projects Tracking Database?

Following are the users who will be directly interacting with the Capital Rehabilitation Projects Tracking system:

- Rehabilitation Projects team, Highways
- Regional and/or District offices
- Other Ministry teams that support reporting activities, for example - GIS

## Why is the Capital Rehabilitation Projects Database Important?

This importance of this project to different stakeholders is given below:

- The benefit to the Ministry is the continued availability of project specific data stored and updated in one location.
- Proper storage of the data will ensure it's availability, and by extension reportability, to the business area through a supportable system.
